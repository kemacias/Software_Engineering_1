## The official Scrum process spells out a lot of detail for how a sprint progresses and who is involved. But here I've described a looser, more general process. What do you imagine are the benefits and drawbacks of sticking with the real Scrum process as opposed to using a variant?

Strict scrum gives everyone super clear responsibilities and expectations as a whole. A variant gives more flexibility and autonomy to individual developers.

## If it's a small team and the Product Owner and Team Lead are the same person, what conflict of interests might arise?

One person would be pushing for more features to keep the client happy while also being responsible for keeping the dev workload sustainable. They might overcommit the team or lower quality standards to hit client deadlines.

## The Retrospective is split out from the Review and Demo. Describe a hypothetical scenario where the Review and Demo went well but the Retrospective did not.

The team delivers every feature and the client loves the demo. But looking back, they see that two devs worked late nights all iteration to cover for unclear requirements and poor communication, and they’re burning out

## The three stand-up questions help the manager keep things on track. How do they also help the developers?

Developers get regular chances to raise blockers early instead of staying stuck. They also see what teammates are working on, so they can coordinate, avoid duplicating work, and ask the right person for help.

## Why is it okay (or even better) to keep Backlog items in the distant future deliberately vague?

Requirements far in the future are likely to change. Spending time detailing them now would probably be wasted effort, so it’s better to refine them once they’re close and more is known.

## What might happen if every developer had their own definition of "done"?

Work would be marked complete while it’s still untested, undeployed, or unapproved. That leads to surprise bugs, inaccurate progress tracking, and friction when someone else picks up “finished” work that isn’t... We've all worked with people who have differing definitions of "done" work.
