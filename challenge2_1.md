### Scenario 1: The Large Order Problem Orson, 20, is part of an underwater hockey team that just won the finals. After the game, he and his teammates are tired and want to celebrate by ordering doner kebabs from a local restaurant using the food ordering app. Since there are many people on the team, Orson orders 32 doner kebabs. The restaurant receives the order and realizes that making 32 kebabs while serving customers in the store will take much longer than normal. The workers want to let Orson know that his order will take longer than expected, but the system only allows them to accept or cancel the order or when its on the way there. They accept the order and begin preparing it, but after waiting for an hour and 22 minutes without receiving an update, Orson becomes frustrated and cancels the order. This causes the restaurant workers to waste the time and food they spent preparing the order. The key risk points: the restaurant has no way to warn customers about unusually long wait times, customers do not receive updates about delays, and customers can cancel an order after the restaurant has already spent significant time preparing it.
